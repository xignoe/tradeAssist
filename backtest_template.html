Backtest — buy the biggest gap to target, sell near target, repeat

Rank the universe by upside to an anchor target, hold the top N equally weighted, sell each name once price closes within 20% of its target, refill from the top of the ranking. Signals measured at the close, filled at the next close, 5 bps per trade.

What this does and does not test read first

The anchor is a trailing 52-week high, not an analyst price target. Only 4 days of point-in-time analyst targets exist (the daily runs started this week), and no reachable source publishes historical consensus targets. Feeding today's targets into past prices would leak the future into the past and manufacture a spectacular, meaningless result — so it is not offered.

What is tested is the same machinery — buy the biggest discount to an anchor, sell on recovery toward it, rotate — with an anchor that was genuinely knowable each day. Treat it as evidence about the strategy's shape, not a forecast of the analyst-target version.

What would break this in live trading

  • Survivorship bias, and it bites this strategy hardest. The universe is today's index membership, so every name shown recovered enough to still be in the index. A "buy what fell furthest" rule is exactly the rule that would have bought the names that never came back — and they are missing here.
  • The exit is not guaranteed. A position only sells when it climbs to within 20% of its anchor. Names that keep falling are held indefinitely, which is why drawdowns run deeper than the index.
  • Concentration. Ten names is not a diversified portfolio; the dispersion between best and worst trade is enormous.
  • Costs modelled are optimistic — 5 bps per trade, no slippage on the small, beaten-down, often illiquid names this strategy selects, no taxes on short holds.
